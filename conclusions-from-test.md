# Conclusions from terraform explorer test

## Take away ideas

* Using AI coding is an iterative process - it is easily missconcieved as being a one and done idea with simeple prompts.


    - Prompting actually needs to be fairly comprehensive to get results, the work seems to shift from the "doing" phase to a planning phase. Ideally I feel I should know what I am asking in advance and how I plan to get there. I should be able to explain this in a simple and logical format.


    - Each time the AI finishes a session the product needs to be tested and evalutated. Unless instructions and plans have been extremely specific and even if you have been specific the AI can still misinterpret desires. As this was a user centric creation the steps need to be walked through and examined. Even though the app might be functional it might not fulfill usablility reuirements. 


* Understanding the tools I am using is a huge accelerator in using AI tools:


    - At the moment this isn't a project that I could create by hand, because of this I cannot personally review the code, I can not advise the agent on how best to create what I am after. I am at the mercy of the AI in creating what I have asked. 


    - As this was a test this was acceptable to me. In use in a testing environment to prototype something as a proof of concept and a way to generate ideas using an "interactive whiteboard" this seems a great way to focus down on what I actually want.


    - However if this was in a workin environment I would want to have a strong understanding of the tools I was using. I would like to be in a position where I could create what I was asking by hand and view critically the crated code. 


* AI creation is not a "fast" process, I am unlikely to create a master prompt and oneshot my goals. My concept of how AI speeds up a task has changed.


    - If a project was going to take 100 hours by hand, it is unlikely that an AI is going to one shot that in an hour and make me 100x more productive.


    - Instead if I need to spend 40 hours learning how to create what I want, 5 hours crafting prompts and creating and curating resources for the AI to use and then another 5 hours of refining the prompts and reviewing the product I have still managed to double my rate of productivity.


* Learning needs to be a key part of AI use and tool generation. 


    - Following from above, gambling on a perfect oneshot prompt might mean I can do in 1 hour what would take me by hand 100 hours. But is luck going to help me on the next 100 hour task? If I spend 50 hours trying to roll for the perfect prompt and fail and spend the next 50 hours on the above approach I have not sped up at all.


    - Spending time early in a project may slow down initial gains in productivity. If we save 10% of the time on early task but develop the skills neccessary to be more productive as a project goes on. 


    - AI can fast track the exploration of blind alleys and dead ends when planning. If I can prototype a basic model and create a critique of it, perhaps I can use it to challenge any assumptions I have built up and change tracks earlier when needed.
